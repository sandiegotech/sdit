# Day 02 — Right Now Is Two Frames Apart

Ten minutes on the page. Twelve photographs of a horse, one word taken apart, one question.

---

## Look

Frame two settles a bet: all four hooves are off the ground. Nobody had ever seen that, because nobody can see one twenty-fifth of a second.</figcaption>
</figure>

Pick any two of the twelve frames. Between them the horse moved some distance in some time. Divide one by the other and you have a speed. That number is real, and it is an average: the horse's speed over that gap, not at any instant inside it.

---

## One idea

Now pick two frames closer together. The average changes a little. Closer still, and it settles. Closer than Muybridge could shoot, and it stops changing at all. The number it settles on is what we mean by the speed right now.

Here is the part worth sitting with. There is no speed in a single frame. Freeze the horse in frame two and ask how fast it is going, and the photograph cannot answer. Speed is a relationship between two moments, never a property of one. "Right now" is not a frame. It is what the pairs of frames agree on as you squeeze them together.

That squeeze has a name, and the catalog gives it: this is Calculus I, and the squeeze is the limit. Day 1 said the whole subject is one move, zoom in. Today you saw what you are zooming in on: not a shape, but a gap between two readings, closing. Everything the course will call a derivative is a speed found this way, and Muybridge found the first one with threads and glass plates. A phone shoots thirty frames a second. Film yourself dropping in, pushing off, swinging a door, and you have the same twelve photographs, with the gap already small.

---

## Carry the question

For the rest of today, carry this one:

**Watch anything move and ask: how close would two photographs have to be before I could call it “now”?**

Answer in a line or a page, or not at all. What you write is kept in your Notebook.

## If you're still curious

Three doors. Open one, or none — they'll be here tomorrow too.

**Read the real thing.** [*Calculus Made Easy*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/33283), chapters 1 and 2: "To deliver you from the preliminary terrors" and "On different degrees of smallness." Six pages. Thompson explains the squeeze with a pocket watch and a slice of bread.

**Watch the horse run.** [Sallie Gardner at a Gallop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sallie_Gardner_at_a_Gallop): the same twelve frames played back, which is how Muybridge's [zoopraxiscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoopraxiscope) became the first moving picture. The gap between frames is the whole illusion of motion, in both directions.

**Ask someone.** Ask a photographer: *"What shutter speed freezes a wave, and what does it lose?"* Listen for the word *blur*. Blur is the average, drawn on the picture.
