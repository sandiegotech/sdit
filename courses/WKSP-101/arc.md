# Arc — WKSP 101 The Workshop

## The course in one paragraph

At the start you have made things, probably, but you have finished few of them and documented none. At the end you have scoped one small build to a size you could finish, finished it, kept a log someone else could follow, taken a critique without flinching, and stood beside the thing and said what it is in two minutes. The catalog register is a project-based fabrication and design practicum, the first of eight; the teaching frame is the bottega — a room with tools and a master in it, where this semester's only law is make anything, and finish it. The eight Workshops are one sequence, and this is the semester that teaches the one skill every later semester assumes: a build has an end, and you are the person who gets it there.

## The made thing

One build, small, finished, and yours — the registry's "shipped project, exhibited publicly." Finished means: it does the thing it was for; nothing on it is held together by intention; someone else can pick it up without you explaining. It is defended at the final Friday bench crit (Day 14's two-minute account, delivered to the room) and it is held for the year-end public exhibition that WKSP 102 carries it to, where it hangs beside the second build. Day 1 has you make anything in ten minutes; Day 2 tells you what the room is; Day 3 scopes the semester's build to a size you can finish and names its done-test; the build starts that week. Day 4 opens the log that runs to Day 15. By Day 15 the build has left the bench.

## The unassisted examination

Day 11's week is the bench test. With the master watching — or, online, the person you have been asking all semester — and with the AI tools, the phone, and the notes closed: cut to a line, make one square joint or one clean edge in your material, and explain the build for two minutes without reading. The log can be polished; the hands cannot. The Workshop certifies hands and judgment, so it has to watch them unaided at least once before the thing is called finished.

## The build and the rotation

**This semester's material.** None is assigned. The room stocks wood, foam, wire, glue, fasteners, and hand tools, and most first builds come out of the small end of board construction — a handplane, a fin, a cruiser deck — because those are finishable in fifteen weeks by someone who has never finished anything. Make anything is the law; finish it is the test.

**The client.** None. You are the client; the brief is yours, which is harder than it sounds and is the point of Day 3.

**The critique method.** Describe before you judge. The work sits on the bench. The maker is silent. For three minutes the room says only what it sees — not what it likes. Then each person asks one question. Then each person names one change. The maker speaks last. The master closes with one instruction, not five. The method is introduced on Day 7 and used every Friday after. Online, you run it with one person over three photographs, or on your own work after a twenty-four-hour wait.

**The exhibition.** The year-end public exhibition closes WKSP 102. This semester's build is held for it. The final bench crit on Day 14 is the dress rehearsal: two minutes, standing beside the thing.

**The documentation discipline.** Three photos and a sentence, every session, dated, in one place. Decisions and failures, not just progress. By Day 12 the log holds one drawing someone could build from. By Day 15 it holds the final account: what it is, who it is for, what fought back, what you would change.

**The rotation across the eight semesters.** The Workshop's materials pass through five of the region's craft traditions, in this order, and every arc states the whole sequence so the eight read as one:

1. **WKSP 101** (Semester 1) — Make anything, and finish it. No assigned tradition; the room's stock pulls most first builds toward the small end of board construction.
2. **WKSP 102** (Semester 2) — Surfboard and skateboard construction. The board goes to the year-end public exhibition.
3. **WKSP 201** (Semester 3) — Amplifier and pedal electronics. The first external client: a musician who will plug it in.
4. **WKSP 202** (Semester 4) — Motorcycle mechanics. A client's machine brought back to running; the last Foundation build and the evidence for the Declaration.
5. **WKSP 301** (Semester 5) — Camera rigs and housings. A client who needs a shot they cannot get; each concentration enters the tradition through its own materials.
6. **WKSP 302** (Semester 6) — Drones and ocean sensors, the sensor end. One instrument that survives a week in saltwater and comes back with data; lighter on purpose, beside the Field Term.
7. **WKSP 401** (Semester 7) — The first-years' build. You lead a WKSP 101 team; the material is whatever they can finish.
8. **WKSP 402** (Semester 8) — Your choice from the five traditions, including the drone end the sixth semester left open. The final build, exhibited beside the capstone.

**What the page is, honestly.** The Workshop is a room with tools and a master in it. The page cannot be that. The fifteen days are the brief, the technique, the critique prompt, and the documentation discipline — the parts that can be written down. A learner working alone online does the smallest real version of the build with what they have: this semester, anything at all, finished, photographed, and shown to one person who did not watch it being made.

## Open questions for Brandon

1. Should the room stock a default first build — a handplane kit, say — for students who freeze at "make anything," or is the freeze part of Day 3's lesson?
2. The final bench crit (Day 14) assumes a master in the room. For the online learner, is the "ask someone" door enough of a stand-in, or should the Institute offer a monthly open crit (video, first come) as the Workshop's one live obligation?
3. Day 11's bench test needs a named technique per material. For wood and foam I have assumed "cut to a line, one square joint or one clean edge." Who sets the equivalent for a learner who chose wire or cloth?
