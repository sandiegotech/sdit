ART 330 — Music Production & Sound Design

The Depths · 4 units · Semester 6 · Depth studio — Media · the Crossing for others

Overview

Recording, synthesis, mixing, and the design of sound itself — the studio treated as the instrument it is. Every fader is a decision and every mix is an argument, and by the end of this course you can say what yours claims. The course stands on the Fourier you already own from Calculus II: a sound is a sum of sines, the organ in Balboa Park is additive synthesis in tin and oak, and an EQ is that same mathematics made into a knob. Here you stop proving it and start hearing it.

This is a depth studio: a control room with a master in it — a producer or mixing engineer with records people have heard — one long block a week, booked session time between, and a brief carried from a release date set on Day 4 to a release on Day 15. The page is the brief. Each day gives you one thing to hear — a 1925 Bessie Smith side cut with one horn and no faders, a bell and a cello, the sound of Jupiter, a loudness-war waveform — one idea, and one question. The room gives you monitors that tell the truth and a critique that plays your rough on a phone and in a car. The AI tools stay open for stem separation and arrangement, and your ears are examined alone once, blind.

It ends in a made thing: a finished piece, released to strangers, with the stems and a production note another producer could learn from.

The Two Registers

  • Catalog register: Audio production — recording, synthesis, mixing, sound design.
  • Teaching frame: The studio as instrument — every fader a decision, every mix an argument.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Capture a sound on purpose — microphone, pattern, distance, room — and say why there. → Workmanship
  2. Shape a sound with synthesis, equalization, dynamics, and placement, hearing each move as the Fourier it is. → Workmanship
  3. Judge a mix against a level-matched reference on three systems, name a processing move blind, and set a balance by ear. → Judgment
  4. Design sound for a picture or an interface — making the inaudible audible — and deliver it to a collaborator’s specification. → Workmanship · Judgment
  5. Place a production decision in the history of recorded sound, from the acoustic horn to the sampler to the stream, and write the production note that makes your record teachable. → Breadth of Mind

Each outcome maps to the five institutional outcomes (Judgment, Command of Intelligence, Workmanship, Articulation, Breadth of Mind) — the assessment spine of the degree and its accreditation evidence file.

The Made Thing

A produced and mixed piece — a track of three to six minutes, or a sound-design cue built for a classmate’s film or interactive piece — recorded, mixed, mastered, and released on a date you set on Day 4. Finished means it plays on the studio monitors, a phone, and a car without surprises; it is released on Bandcamp or screened with the work it was made for; and the stems, the recall sheet, and a one-page production note exist. The exhibition is a listening session for invited strangers. The release and the note enter your portfolio for the Year 3 gate.

How You’re Assessed

  • The brief, the reference record, and the release date, set on Day 4 and held.
  • Three critiques: the rough on three speakers (Day 8), the blind test and the balance (Day 11), and mix lock heard start to finish as a listener would (Day 12).
  • The unassisted examination (Day 11): no analyzer, no plug-in, no reference — five before-and-after clips, name the move; six stems, twenty minutes, a balance by ear and a hand-written recall sheet. The ear is the instrument the institution certifies, so the ear is examined alone.
  • The release, on time, with credits, stems, recall sheet, and production note.
  • Everything lands in your portfolio, reviewed against the five outcomes at the end-of-year gate.
