# MATH 220 — Probability & Statistics

**Mathematics & Science** · 4 units · Semester 4 · Required core — everyone takes it

## Overview

Uncertainty is the native condition of the AI age, and this is its grammar. Every model's output is a probability; every surf report is a distribution; every bet, poll, A/B test, and forecast is a claim about what you do not know, stated precisely enough to be wrong. This course teaches you to count the ways a thing can happen, update a belief as evidence arrives, price a bet, put an honest error bar on a measurement, tell a real effect from noise, fit a line through buoy data and know what it cannot say — and then to make forecasts in public and be scored on them.

Taught as **Forecasting & Games**: swell models, prediction markets, poker, A/B tests. Every concept arrives physical before formal — you count outs at the table before you see conditional probability, watch beads pile into a bell on a Galton board before you see the normal distribution, time the period at Scripps Pier ten times before you see a confidence interval. The discipline the course is built around is the oldest one in forecasting: write the number down, wait, and look.

One day a week, about ten minutes a sitting, plus a forecast posted every week from the sixth. AI tools stay open — reading a model's output as a distribution is half the subject — and your own unassisted reasoning with probability is examined, because a model will produce a number for any question and only you can say whether it was the right question. The course ends the way every SDIT course ends: in a made thing — a public forecast, tracked and scored — defended before the room and the ledger.

## The Two Registers

- **Catalog register:** Probability theory, distributions, inference, regression. Transfer-legible introductory probability and statistics.
- **Teaching frame:** Forecasting and games — swell models, prediction markets, poker, A/B tests. The native mathematics of the AI age.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course you can:

1. Count outcomes, compute conditional probabilities, and update a belief with Bayes' theorem on a real case — a hand of cards, a buoy reading, a test result. → *Judgment*
2. Work with random variables and the binomial, Poisson, normal, and extreme-value distributions; compute expectations and variances; and say what a reported statistic — a significant wave height, a conversion rate — is a statistic of. → *Judgment · Command of Intelligence*
3. Form and interpret a confidence interval, read and criticize a hypothesis test, and design an honest A/B test. → *Judgment*
4. Fit and criticize a regression — slope, residuals, what it cannot claim — and distinguish correlation from cause. → *Command of Intelligence*
5. Make probabilistic forecasts, score them with Brier and log scores, read your own calibration curve, and evaluate a model's forecasts the same way; demonstrate the underlying reasoning unassisted in periodic written examinations. → *Command of Intelligence · Judgment*

*Each outcome maps to the five institutional outcomes (Judgment, Command of Intelligence, Workmanship, Articulation, Breadth of Mind) — the assessment spine of the degree and its accreditation evidence file.*

## The Made Thing

A forecast with skin in the game — made, tracked, and scored in public. From the sixth week you post weekly probabilistic forecasts to a shared public ledger — the surf height at a named spot on a named day, the hour the marine layer lifts, an election, a market, a metric from a running A/B test — each with a stated probability and a resolution source named in advance. They are scored as they resolve. At the end you present at least eight resolved forecasts, your score, your calibration curve, the model behind them, and a public write-up of your worst miss. The ledger stays public.

## How You're Assessed

- **Weekly problem sets and weekly forecasts** — AI tools open; what's graded is your reasoning, your base rates, your updates, and your honesty about uncertainty.
- **The scored forecast**, defended before the room and the ledger.
- **Unassisted written examinations** — probability, distributions, and intervals after the first half; inference, regression, and scoring after the second. AI-native, not AI-dependent.
- Everything lands in your **portfolio**, reviewed against the five outcomes at the end-of-year gate.
