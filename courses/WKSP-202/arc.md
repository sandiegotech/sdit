# Arc — WKSP 202 The Workshop

## The course in one paragraph

At the start you have shipped three builds, one of them for a client; by the end you have brought a client's motorcycle — or, online, a bicycle, a scooter, a small engine — from not running to running, by diagnosis rather than by guessing, to a torque spec and a service log the next mechanic can read, and you have laid your four Foundation builds on one table and said, with evidence, which part of the work your hands reach for. The catalog register is the fourth course of the eight-semester fabrication and design practicum; the teaching frame is a client project carried to the end of the Foundation — the body of made work you declare your concentration from. The machine is the client's. The Declaration is yours.

## The made thing

A client project, shipped, and a portfolio review ahead of the Declaration. The project: one motorcycle, belonging to a named rider, brought from a fault they describe to a ride they take, with a service log, a torque record, and the parts and hours accounted. Finished means: it starts, runs, stops, and the client has ridden it; every fastener you touched is at spec and on the record; the log tells the next mechanic what was done and why. Day 1 names the tradition and the client; Day 3 writes the brief as symptoms, not parts; Day 2 and Day 4 teach the machine before anyone touches it; the wrenches come out in Week 4. The portfolio review is Day 14: four builds on one table, and the question of where your hands went. By Day 15 the machine is back with its rider and you have named a concentration from experience.

## The unassisted examination

Day 11's week. The master puts a fault in a running machine — a pulled plug lead, a pinched fuel line, a loose ground — and you find it with your ears, your nose, your hands, a meter, and nothing else: no manual, no phone, no AI. Say what you hear, say what you suspect, test one thing, and say what you found. Why this: a motorcycle can be fixed by replacing parts until it runs. The Workshop certifies that you can reason from a symptom to a cause, which is the whole of Judgment in two cylinders.

## The build and the rotation

**This semester's material.** Motorcycle mechanics — the third tradition of the rotation and the first machine with every concentration inside it: mechanical (engine, drivetrain, chassis), electrical (ignition, charging, lights), fluid (fuel, oil, brakes, cooling), and finish (paint, chrome, the thing the rider is proud of). Teams of three to four take one machine. Online, the smallest real version is a bicycle, scooter, mower, or outboard belonging to someone else, brought back to running by the same method.

**The client.** A rider with a machine that does not run right — a student, a neighbor, a shop that will lend a customer's bike with permission. The client describes symptoms on Day 3, comes to the lift for the crit on Day 8, and rides the machine on Day 13.

**The critique method.** Describe before you judge, at the lift, with the machine running or not. Added this semester: the crit is cross-disciplinary by design. The team member who did the wiring describes the carburetion; the one who painted the tank describes the brakes. If you cannot describe the part you did not do, the team has a documentation problem.

**The exhibition.** None; the deliverable is the ride. The portfolio review on Day 14 is the semester's public moment: four builds, one table, the room describing where your hands went before you declare.

**The documentation discipline.** Three photos and a sentence, continuing the log. Added: the symptom brief (Day 3), the service log with torque values (Day 9), the fluids record (Day 6), the fault tree from the exam (Day 11), and the portfolio page — one page per Foundation build, four pages, for the Declaration (Day 14).

**The rotation across the eight semesters.**

1. **WKSP 101** (Semester 1) — Make anything, and finish it. No assigned tradition; the room's stock pulls most first builds toward the small end of board construction.
2. **WKSP 102** (Semester 2) — Surfboard and skateboard construction. The board goes to the year-end public exhibition.
3. **WKSP 201** (Semester 3) — Amplifier and pedal electronics. The first external client: a musician who will plug it in.
4. **WKSP 202** (Semester 4) — Motorcycle mechanics. A client's machine brought back to running; the last Foundation build and the evidence for the Declaration.
5. **WKSP 301** (Semester 5) — Camera rigs and housings. A client who needs a shot they cannot get; each concentration enters the tradition through its own materials.
6. **WKSP 302** (Semester 6) — Drones and ocean sensors, the sensor end. One instrument that survives a week in saltwater and comes back with data; lighter on purpose, beside the Field Term.
7. **WKSP 401** (Semester 7) — The first-years' build. You lead a WKSP 101 team; the material is whatever they can finish.
8. **WKSP 402** (Semester 8) — Your choice from the five traditions, including the drone end the sixth semester left open. The final build, exhibited beside the capstone.

**What the page is, honestly.** The Workshop is a lift, a torque wrench, a meter, a parts washer, a manual, and a mechanic who has heard that noise before. The page cannot be that. The fifteen days carry the brief, the method of diagnosis, the technique in order, the critique prompt, and the discipline of the service log. A learner working alone online does the smallest real version: someone else's bicycle, scooter, or mower, from a symptom they describe to a ride they take, with a log the next person could follow.
