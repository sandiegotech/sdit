# SIG 401 — How to Build a Signal

**Culture & Signal** · 1 unit · Semester 8 · Required core — everyone takes it

## Overview

Four years of taking culture in; now you put something back out and watch what the world does with it. This is the last Culture & Signal unit and the capstone in media practice: taste, curation, and distribution, learned from people who did it before there were platforms. Hume's two wine tasters, laughed at until the key turned up at the bottom of the cask. Paine giving up his profits so *Common Sense* could be reprinted by anyone, and selling 120,000 copies in three months. Franklin's almanac, Dürer's print business, Roosevelt at the same hour every week, a punk zine run off on a copier, Bruce Brown renting a theater in Wichita in winter to prove a surf film could play anywhere. Then the AI era, where the machine is a reader of your signal too — and the man who wrote the book on propaganda, because by the end of this course you know how to move people and have to decide what you will refuse to do with that.

Every day puts the real thing on the page — the engraving, the pamphlet, the recording, a Navy ship sliding into San Diego Bay at NASSCO on the day it ships. The course runs alongside your capstone and must not be it; it is the signal you build around something you believe in.

One day a week, about ten minutes a sitting, and the rest of the week making and shipping. The course ends in a made thing: a real cultural artifact — a zine, a newsletter, a record, a radio hour, a show in a real room — shipped to a real audience outside the building, with the numbers, defended out loud before an outside examiner.

## The Two Registers

- **Catalog register:** Capstone in media practice — curation, distribution, audience.
- **Teaching frame:** Taste, curation, distribution in the AI era. You ship a real cultural artifact to a real audience.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course you can:

1. Defend a judgment of taste by pointing at something in the work, and curate a set of things in an order that tells someone how to look. → *Judgment*
2. Design a cultural artifact for a specific channel — its edition, its schedule, its carriers — and ship it on the date you said. → *Workmanship*
3. Build an audience you own alongside one you rent, and prove demand in a room you control before asking a channel at scale to believe it. → *Workmanship · Judgment*
4. Report the numbers honestly — with denominators, carriers named, and one effect recorded — in a one-page account written unaided and read aloud. → *Articulation*
5. State what you will not do to get attention, and why, in a way you would say to your audience's face. → *Judgment · Articulation*

*Each outcome maps to the five institutional outcomes (Judgment, Command of Intelligence, Workmanship, Articulation, Breadth of Mind) — the assessment spine of the degree and its accreditation evidence file.*

## The Made Thing

A real cultural artifact shipped to a real audience — with the numbers. Accepted forms: a printed zine or small book with a stated run; a newsletter with at least four issues sent on schedule; a record or mixtape released; a radio hour or podcast season; a screening, show, reading, or exhibition in a real room with a door count. It must reach people outside the cohort through one channel you control and one you do not, on the date you state in the fourth week. Finished means shipped, counted with denominators, the carriers named, one effect recorded, and the one-page launch account written with the tools closed. It is defended on the last day before the cohort and an outside examiner who has shipped culture in this city. It enters your portfolio beside the capstone and is read at the final gate.

## How You're Assessed

- **Weekly build notes**: the scope, the first unit, the list, the ship date — each checked against what you said the week before.
- **The artifact**, shipped on the stated date, and **the numbers**, counted and traced in the two weeks after.
- **An unassisted examination** in the fourteenth week: the one-page launch account — what it is, who it was for, the numbers with denominators, who carried it, what it made anyone do, what you would change — written in forty-five minutes with the tools closed and read aloud in five. AI tools are open for making the artifact; the institution certifies the human, and a graduate explains their own numbers in their own words.
- **The defense** on the last day, before an outside examiner.
- Everything lands in your **portfolio**, reviewed against the five outcomes at the final gate.
