# Arc — SIG 401 How to Build a Signal

## The course in one paragraph

Four years of taking culture in; this is the semester you put something back out. At the start you have taste you cannot fully defend and an audience of friends. At the end you have shipped a real cultural artifact — a zine, a newsletter, a record, a radio hour, a screening, a small book, a show in a real room — to a real audience outside the cohort, through one channel you control and one you do not, and you can stand up and say what it was, who it reached, the numbers with their denominators, who carried it, what it made anyone do, and what you would change. The catalog register is the capstone in media practice: curation, distribution, audience. The teaching frame is taste, curation, and distribution in the AI era. The material runs from Hume's wine tasters to Paine's pamphlet, Franklin's almanac, Dürer's print business, a Tijuana-born radio signal, a Navy ship sliding into the bay at NASSCO, and the man who wrote the book on propaganda — because you are about to know how to move people, and the course ends by asking what you will refuse to do with that.

## The made thing

A real cultural artifact, shipped. The forms accepted: a printed zine or small book with a stated run; a newsletter with at least four issues sent on schedule; a record or mixtape released; a radio hour or podcast season; a screening, show, reading, or exhibition in a real room with a door count; or a curated object the learner can defend as one of these. It must be made for an audience outside the cohort and the Institute, and reach them through one channel the learner controls (a list, a room, a table, a run of copies) and one they do not (a shop, a feed, a station, a venue's calendar).

"Finished" means it shipped on the date the learner stated on Day 4; the numbers are in hand with denominators (copies handed out of copies printed; subscribers and open rate; attendance against capacity; plays and listeners; sales); the carriers are named (who passed it on); and one effect is recorded — something a named person did because of it. "Finished" also means the learner has written the one-page launch account unaided and read it aloud.

Day 1 announces it. Day 4 scopes it: form, edition or run, audience, the two channels, and the ship date, said out loud. Day 7 starts it: the first unit made or the list opened. Day 11 is ship day. Days 12 and 13 count and trace. Day 14 is the account, written with the tools closed. Day 15 is the defense before the cohort and an outside examiner. It enters the portfolio and is read at the final gate beside the capstone, and it must not be the capstone — it may be a signal built around it.

## The unassisted examination

Week 14, Day 14 itself, forty-five minutes, tools closed. The learner writes the one-page launch account — what it is, who it was for, the numbers with denominators, who carried it, what it made anyone do, and what they would change — and then reads it aloud in five minutes. Articulation is a listed outcome of this course and the account is where it is certified. A graduate of this institution explains their own numbers in their own words; that cannot be delegated to a model and the course observes it directly.

## The signal

The real artifact shipped is the learner's own, in the form they chose on Day 4, through two channels — one owned, one not. The audience is real and outside the building: readers who are not classmates, a room with a door, subscribers who did not have to sign up, a shop that did not have to stock it. Movement is measured on Days 12 and 13 with denominators attached and the carriers named: how many of how many; who passed it on, by name where possible; what anyone did. The AI-era test from Day 10 is applied as well: can the artifact be found and summarized accurately by a machine, and does it still need a person to want it? A signal that reached forty people and was carried by three of them to forty more is a signal. One that reached forty thousand and moved no one is noise with good numbers, and the course says so.

## Open questions for Brandon

1. **The relationship to the capstone.** The arc requires the signal not to *be* the CAP 402 work but allows it to be built around it (a zine about the thesis, a show for the record). Is that the rule you want, or should the signal be entirely separate so that a fourth-year ships two different things?
2. **The outside examiner.** Day 15 puts an outside examiner in the room — someone who has shipped culture in this city. Who is the first name on that list: a label owner, a promoter, a newsletter writer, a bookseller?
3. **The Institute as a channel.** Should the Institute's own list and site count as the "channel you control," or must the learner build their own list from zero? The first is kinder; the second is the course.
