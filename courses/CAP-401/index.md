# CAP 401 — Thesis / Venture Capstone I

**Field Term & Capstone** · 2 units · Semester 7 · Required core — everyone takes it

## Overview

The whole degree converges here: one original work, a year in the making, in one of three accepted forms — a research thesis; an engineered system that works; a venture that exists, incorporated, with users. This semester you choose the form, argue the scope, fix the defense date, and start building it for real. Next semester (CAP 402) you finish it and defend it in public. In HUM 402 you argue it against everything you have read in four years.

This is not a subject course. Nothing here is taught. The fifteen days are fifteen weekly checkpoints on a year-long work — a place to stand once a week and look at what you have made since the last one — and each carries one document, photograph, or passage from someone who did the thing: Darwin's notebook page that says "I think," Wilbur Wright's letter asking the Smithsonian for everything already known, Franklin's one-page proposal that founded a society still standing, Leonardo's first dated drawing. And one question to carry into the work. AI tools are open throughout; the delegation log is part of the record, and the proposal's one page and the midterm state of the work are written with the tools closed.

The course ends the way every SDIT course ends: in a made thing — an accepted proposal, and the first working piece of the thing itself, shown to peers and adviser in Week 14.

## The Two Registers

- **Catalog register:** Capstone I — proposal and scoping of an original work; execution begins under faculty advising.
- **Teaching frame:** Three accepted forms: a research thesis; an engineered system that works; a venture that exists — incorporated, with users. This semester: the proposal, the scope, and the first real work.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course you can:

1. Choose the form of an original work — thesis, system, or venture — and argue the choice from three years of made work and a survey of what already exists. → *Judgment · Breadth of Mind*
2. Write a one-page capstone proposal — claim, scope, what finished means, calendar, precedent — and defend it in an oral scoping conversation without notes. → *Articulation · Judgment*
3. Direct machine intelligence in a long work, log what it did, verify what it returned, and build the instrument that checks it. → *Command of Intelligence*
4. Make the first working piece of the thing: a chapter with real data, a system running end to end on real input, or a venture with a first user who is not your friend. → *Workmanship*
5. Keep a dated record of the work that an examiner could read cold and trust. → *Articulation · Workmanship*

*Each outcome maps to the five institutional outcomes (Judgment, Command of Intelligence, Workmanship, Articulation, Breadth of Mind) — the assessment spine of the degree and its accreditation evidence file.*

## The Made Thing

An **accepted capstone proposal** — one page naming the form, the claim or need in a sentence, what finished means as a checkable list, what is out of scope, the precedents, how machine intelligence is used and verified, and the calendar to a fixed defense date, signed by the adviser and a second reader after an oral scoping conversation — and the **first working piece of the thing itself**: a chapter with data, a system that runs end to end badly on a real input in front of someone, or a venture that is legally formed with one real user, demonstrated to peers in Week 14. Both enter your portfolio. A learner working alone online runs this as a self-set work with a found adviser — one person with a body of work in the field who will read a page a week — and the same page, the same piece, and the same fixed date.

## How You're Assessed

- **Fifteen weekly checkpoints**, each with the week's document in hand: the paragraph, the form, the precedent survey, the page, the scope, the calendar, and so on. Missing a checkpoint is the signal; the adviser calls.
- **The proposal**, accepted with two signatures after an oral scoping conversation. The one page is written **unaided** first — one sitting, tools closed — and read beside the assisted draft; where they differ, the unaided page is the one that is true.
- **The delegation log** — what machine intelligence did, what you checked, what it got wrong.
- **The midterm state of the work**, one page, unaided, followed by the adviser's cut-or-keep review.
- **The first working piece**, demonstrated to peers and adviser in Week 14, with written feedback kept in the record.
- Everything lands in your **portfolio**, reviewed against the five outcomes at the end-of-year gate.
