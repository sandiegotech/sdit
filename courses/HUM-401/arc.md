# Arc — HUM 401 The California Canon

## The course in one paragraph

At the end of this course you can read the place you live as a text. You can say what Steinbeck, Chandler, Didion, and Thompson each did with a sentence and borrow the move; you can trace California's one plot — water, land, and who gets them — from a hide house on the San Diego shore in 1835 to a backyard pool in Venice in 1976; and you can argue that surf and skate cinema is the state's founding myth, with evidence. The catalog register is literature of California — Steinbeck, Didion, Chandler, Thompson. The teaching frame is surf and skate cinema as founding myth; the place SDIT lives, read as a text. You will write a piece of it yourself.

## The made thing

**The Square Mile.** A piece of California literature of the learner's own, about one square mile of San Diego County, made in direct response to one of the four authors. An essay of 1,500 to 2,500 words, a short film under ten minutes, a recorded piece, or a designed object with a written text attached. It is read or exhibited *at the place it is about*, before at least two witnesses, and then defended in seminar.

*Finished* means: it names the place; it holds one argument about it; it borrows one named technique from one of the four (Steinbeck's intercalary chapter, Chandler's weather, Didion's exact detail that refuses to conclude, Thompson's narrator as instrument) and says so; and it can be read aloud in under ten minutes. The defense asks what the place did while you read.

Day 1 introduces it (the oldest San Diego passage in American letters is a work report; the place is already a text). Day 6 scopes it (name your square mile and its oldest thing). Day 5 shows the model (Guthrie's "Tom Joad" as a made response to a book and a film). Days 11 to 13 draft. Day 14 examines the learner unaided. Day 15 is the reading at the water and the defense. By Day 15 every learner has stood at a place and read it aloud.

## The unassisted examination

Day 14, one sitting, forty-five minutes, tools closed: four hundred words about your square mile in the manner of one of the four authors, named at the top. Articulation is this course's first outcome, and style is only yours when you can produce it with no model in the room. The writer is the instrument.

## Rights and sources

- **All four named authors are in copyright.** Steinbeck, Chandler, Didion, Thompson: quotes under fifteen words only, doors to buy or borrow (Penguin, Vintage, FSG). Wolfe and Stecyk likewise. Steinbeck's *The Harvest Gypsies* (1936 newspaper series) may be in copyright; treat it as such.
- **The public-domain spine** is real and strong: Dana (1840), Turner (1893), Austin (1903), London (1907), Yeats (1920), and the FSA photographs (Lange, 1936–37), Watkins (1865), the Bain Collection (c. 1912), HABS, NASA, NPS, and BLM photographs. Confirm Gutenberg catalog numbers (#2055, #22994, #365, #2512, and the *Michael Robartes and the Dancer* volume) before Day 1 is written.
- **Films on page:** *The Gold Rush* (1925) and *Detour* (1945) are public domain in the U.S.; the 1942 re-release of *The Gold Rush* with narration is not. Use Internet Archive items of the original cuts and confirm them. Every other film is a door: *The Grapes of Wrath*, *The Big Sleep*, *Chinatown*, *Gimme Shelter*, *Big Wednesday*, *Dogtown and Z-Boys*.
- **Albums** are doors. Guthrie's 1940 recordings are not public domain (sound recordings 1923–1946 enter the public domain in 2041); Smithsonian Folkways is the buy link. Official channels to confirm for embeds: Rhino/Warner (Ronstadt, The Doors), Anti- (Waits), and Dick Dale's estate; if uncertain, link out.
- **Commons images to confirm by file page:** the Old Point Loma Lighthouse (NPS), Watkins's Yosemite, the 1913 Aqueduct opening (LAPL — confirm the PD statement), the Salinas Valley view (CC), the HABS Angels Flight photograph, the Haight-Ashbury sign (CC), the NASA Central Valley image, the BLM Mojave photograph, Duke Kahanamoku c. 1912 (Bain), the Windansea shack (CC), the Cabrillo NM view (NPS). ## Open questions for Brandon

1. Is "one square mile of San Diego County" the right unit for the made thing, or should the learner be allowed any California place they can stand in? San Diego keeps the Institute's laboratory in the frame; the state-wide version lets a learner from Fresno go home.
2. Does Windansea get named as the course's standing site — Wolfe's pump house, the shack, the break — for Day 13 and the Day 15 readings? It is a real place with its own locals, and the Institute would be sending students to it.
3. Fifth unit's album: Dick Dale's *Surfers' Choice* (the sound was invented here) or the Minutemen's *Double Nickels on the Dime* (San Pedro, 1984, the skate-punk record)? The arc takes Dale for the founding myth; the Minutemen would be the honest skate pairing.
