# HUM 401 — The California Canon

**The Canon** · 2 units · Semester 7 · Required core — everyone takes it

## Overview

In 1835 a Harvard student named Richard Henry Dana spent months curing cattle hides on the beach below Point Loma and wrote it down. That is the oldest San Diego passage in American literature, and it is a work report. California entered letters as labor and place before it became a dream, and this course reads the place SDIT lives in as a text — from Dana's hide house to Steinbeck's valley, Chandler's rain, Didion's water, Thompson's wave, and a sewage pump house at Windansea where Tom Wolfe found a culture being invented.

It is taught by the Double Feature Method: each unit pairs **the text + a film + an album** as one conversation. *The Grapes of Wrath* beside Ford's film and Woody Guthrie, who saw the film and wrote the whole novel into a ballad by morning. Chandler beside Bogart and Tom Waits. Didion and Thompson beside *Gimme Shelter* and The Doors. And the state's founding myth — surf and skate cinema — read as what it is: the frontier that ran out at the water and turned around.

You are in your fourth year and you have shaped a board, forecast a swell, and filmed a session. Now you write the place. The course ends in a made thing: a piece of California literature of your own, about one square mile of San Diego County, read aloud at the place it is about and then defended in seminar.

## The Two Registers

- **Catalog register:** Literature of California — Steinbeck, Didion, Chandler, Thompson.
- **Teaching frame:** Surf and skate cinema as founding myth; the place SDIT lives, read as a text — and written into by the learner.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course you can:

1. Read a passage of Steinbeck, Chandler, Didion, or Thompson closely, name the technique at work in the sentence, and borrow it deliberately in your own. → *Articulation*
2. Trace California's plot — water, land, and who gets them — across a public-domain spine (Dana, Turner, Austin, London) and the four named authors, with evidence from each. → *Breadth of Mind*
3. Argue, with evidence from film, music, and text, that surf and skate cinema is the state's founding myth — and say where the argument is weak. → *Breadth of Mind · Articulation*
4. Write four hundred words about a real place in the manner of a named author, in one sitting, with the tools closed. → *Articulation*
5. Make a piece of California literature of your own about one square mile, read it at the place, and defend it in seminar. → *Articulation · Breadth of Mind*

*Each outcome maps to the five institutional outcomes (Judgment, Command of Intelligence, Workmanship, Articulation, Breadth of Mind) — the assessment spine of the degree and its accreditation evidence file.*

## The Made Thing

The Square Mile: a piece of California literature of your own about one square mile of San Diego County, made in direct response to one of the four authors — an essay of 1,500 to 2,500 words, a short film under ten minutes, a recorded piece, or a designed object with a written text. It names the place, holds one argument about it, borrows one named technique and says so, and can be read aloud in under ten minutes. It is read or exhibited at the place itself before witnesses, then defended in seminar. It enters your portfolio and is reviewed at the annual gate.

## How You're Assessed

- **Short written responses**, line-edited and revised — a sentence taken apart, a technique borrowed, a place described.
- **The Square Mile**, read at the place it is about and defended out loud in seminar at the end of the course.
- **One unassisted examination**, tools closed: four hundred words on your square mile in the manner of one of the four. Style is only yours when you can produce it with no model in the room.
- Everything lands in your **portfolio**, reviewed against the five outcomes at the end-of-year gate.
