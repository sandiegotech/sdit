# Arc — HUM 202 The Industrial Mind

## The course in one paragraph

At the start, the learner knows the nineteenth century as steam engines and top hats. At the end, they can state the four ideas that century left running inside every modern maker: that what you make may turn and ask you for an account (Shelley), that living things were made by no one and change without a plan (Darwin), that the factory makes the worker as surely as the worker makes the product (Marx), and that with no maker above you the remaining task is to make yourself (Nietzsche). The catalog register is nineteenth-century thought: Marx, Darwin, Nietzsche, Shelley. The teaching frame is the Double Feature, opening with *Frankenstein* beside *Blade Runner* and the birth of electronic music, the century that invented the machine and the anxiety about it. The learner leaves able to read a modern engineering decision as a nineteenth-century argument continued, and to say which side they are on. This is the last Canon course of the Foundation, and it ends the week the learner declares a concentration.

## The made thing

A made response to one of the century's four questions, in one of four forms: an essay (1,200 to 2,000 words), a short film (under six minutes), a recorded piece, or a designed object with a one-page note. This semester's extra constraint comes from the teaching frame: the response must be, or must contain, something the learner made that could act without them. A piece of electronic music built from a patch that runs on its own, a mechanism, a script, a generative image, a rule set. The learner then answers Shelley's question about it in writing: what do you owe it, and what does it owe you? "Finished" means the thing exists, runs or plays without the maker present, names its text, and carries the written answer. It is defended in seminar the week after Day 15: three minutes to present, seven of questions, peers first. It enters the portfolio and is read against Judgment, Articulation, and Breadth of Mind at the end-of-year gate, the same week as the Declaration.

Day 3 introduces it: Shelley wrote the novel at eighteen from the live science of her day, and the course tells the learner they will make something and answer for it. Day 8 scopes it: on the factory floor of *Metropolis*, the learner names the question, the form, and the thing that will act on its own, in three sentences. Day 11 starts it: the word "robot" means forced labor, and a first version of the thing that runs is due within the week. Day 15 sets the defense on the Arctic ice where the novel ends.

## The unassisted examination

In the week of Day 10, thirty minutes, tools closed: state Marx's argument in the chapter on the working day, including what the factory inspectors found and what the fight was actually over, then give an honest account of your own working day this week, hour by hour, and say where the two meet. Marx is the right text for this because the chapter is built from evidence, and the degree needs to see the learner handle a factual argument and then apply it to their own time without a tool filling in the hours. The Day 15 defense is also unaided.

## Open questions for Brandon

1. The catalog's "birth of electronic music" is a category, not a record. I chose the Barrons' *Forbidden Planet* score (1956) because it is the Frankenstein story told in solder: circuits built to live and burn out. The alternatives are Kraftwerk (which this arc gives to Marx) or a musique concrète record. Which record is the fixed pairing?
2. *Groundhog Day* for Nietzsche rather than *2001: A Space Odyssey*. The eternal recurrence is the film's plot, and it leaves HAL to HUM 301. If you would rather have *2001*, it also serves Darwin's bone-to-satellite cut, and the arc can swap it in with *Hunky Dory* unchanged.
3. The made thing this semester must be or contain something that acts without the learner. That is a harder brief than the other three Canon courses, chosen because the course's question is about made things that act. If it is too much to ask beside the Declaration and CS 220, the constraint can drop to a recommendation.
