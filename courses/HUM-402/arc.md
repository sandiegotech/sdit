# Arc — HUM 402 The Long View

## The course in one paragraph

At the end of this course you can place your capstone in a four-year conversation. You can say what it owes to at least one text from each of the eight Canon semesters, and what it argues against. You can say what the long record of civilizations rising and declining — Polybius, Gibbon, Ibn Khaldun, Tainter — tells you about whether what you made will matter, and for how long. And you can answer a cold question from any of those texts without notes. The catalog register is capstone colloquium — civilizational rise and decline; thesis work presented against the whole sequence. The teaching frame is your capstone, argued against everything you have read in four years. This course runs in the same semester as CAP 402; the colloquium is the Canon's half of the defense.

## The made thing

**The Colloquium Presentation.** Twenty minutes before the seminar and one outside examiner, given in Week 15, placing the capstone — thesis, system, or venture — against the whole sequence. It travels with a one-page *lineage sheet*: eight lines, one per Canon semester, each naming one text and saying in a sentence what the capstone owes it or where it argues back. The presentation is followed by an examiners' round of three questions, one of them drawn cold from a passage read in an earlier course.

*Finished* means: given, in time, with the lineage sheet complete and every line pointing at a passage the learner can open. The presentation is defended by answering the three questions; the form is the learner's (slides, a reading, a demonstration, a recording), and the test is whether a person who read none of the eight courses would leave knowing what the capstone is and why four years of reading were the right preparation for it.

Day 1 introduces it (the capstone in one sentence, addressed to someone not yet born). Day 2 scopes it (the lineage sheet). Four rounds rehearse it: Day 4 (one minute), Day 7 (against Year 2), Day 10 (against Year 3), Day 13 (the full run with the sheet). Day 14 examines the learner unaided. Day 15 is the colloquium. By Day 15 every learner has given it, and the sequence has closed where it opened.

## The unassisted examination

Day 14: five minutes, no notes. The learner stands and speaks for five minutes on the capstone against the sequence — no slides, no notes, no tools. Then they draw one passage at random from a set of sixteen (two per Canon semester, all read before) and answer one question on it. The degree certifies the human. The last examination in the Canon should be the human speaking alone about what they made and what they read.
