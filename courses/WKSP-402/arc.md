# Arc — WKSP 402 The Workshop

## The course in one paragraph

At the start you have shipped seven builds and led a team through an eighth; by the end you have chosen one of the five traditions — boards, amplifier and pedal electronics, motorcycle mechanics, camera rigs and housings, drones and ocean sensors — built one last thing in it with four years of hands, hung it beside your defended capstone at the final public exhibition, and written the account of eight semesters of making in your own words. The catalog register is the eighth and last course of the eight-semester fabrication and design practicum; the teaching frame is the final exhibition — the last build, shown in public beside the capstone. Eight semesters of making end in the room they started in.

## The made thing

The final build, exhibited publicly. One thing, in a tradition you choose, finished to the standard of the whole sequence: it does what it is for; nothing is held by intention; a stranger can read its placard and use it or watch it work; it hangs beside the capstone with the seven builds before it on a single wall of pages. Finished also means defended: on exhibition night, outside examiners ask about it, and you answer. Day 1 chooses the material; Day 2 writes the last brief you will write for yourself; Days 3 and 4 open the two doors — the drone end the rotation left open, and the second pass through any tradition; the build runs Weeks 5 to 12. By Day 15 it is on the wall, the eight-semester account is written, and the room that started this is full of people.

## The unassisted examination

Day 11's week, tools closed, outside examiners in the room: the final bench test in your chosen material — the technique your tradition's master names — executed with someone watching; then the oral defense of the build, start to finish, with questions, no notes. Why this: the capstone is defended in another room. The Workshop's last exam is the one that cannot be passed by anything but four years of hands, and it is given before the public sees the thing, so that what hangs on the wall has already been examined.

## The build and the rotation

**This semester's material.** Your choice from the five traditions. Two doors are open that were not before: the drone end of the fifth tradition, which WKSP 302 introduced and left; and the second pass — returning to a tradition from an earlier semester with four years of hands and building the thing you could not have built then. Teams may be of one. Teams may be of three concentrations. The master of the chosen tradition is your master this semester.

**The client.** You, for the last time — with the public and the outside examiners as the people you answer to. Students whose capstone is a venture or an engineered system may build the Workshop thing for a client of the capstone's, if the brief stays one thing, fifteen weeks, defended on its own.

**The critique method.** Describe before you judge, in its final form: the public defense. Day 7 brings an outside eye to the bench — someone from the tradition who has never seen your work — and the rule holds that they describe before they judge and you speak last. On Day 15 the examiners ask and you answer; the method you learned on Day 7 of WKSP 101 is what gets you through.

**The exhibition.** The final public exhibition, beside the defended capstone. Day 10 is the hang; Day 13 is the opening; Day 15 is the close. The seven previous builds are present as a wall of pages — one page each, from the portfolio — so that the last build is read as the eighth of a sequence.

**The documentation discipline.** Three photos and a sentence, to the end. Added: the last brief (Day 2), the final log with the archive in mind (Day 8), the placard for eight semesters — one paragraph per build, eight paragraphs (Day 12) — and the account of eight semesters (Day 14), which Cellini would have called a life.

**The rotation across the eight semesters.**

1. **WKSP 101** (Semester 1) — Make anything, and finish it. No assigned tradition; the room's stock pulls most first builds toward the small end of board construction.
2. **WKSP 102** (Semester 2) — Surfboard and skateboard construction. The board goes to the year-end public exhibition.
3. **WKSP 201** (Semester 3) — Amplifier and pedal electronics. The first external client: a musician who will plug it in.
4. **WKSP 202** (Semester 4) — Motorcycle mechanics. A client's machine brought back to running; the last Foundation build and the evidence for the Declaration.
5. **WKSP 301** (Semester 5) — Camera rigs and housings. A client who needs a shot they cannot get; each concentration enters the tradition through its own materials.
6. **WKSP 302** (Semester 6) — Drones and ocean sensors, the sensor end. One instrument that survives a week in saltwater and comes back with data; lighter on purpose, beside the Field Term.
7. **WKSP 401** (Semester 7) — The first-years' build. You lead a WKSP 101 team; the material is whatever they can finish.
8. **WKSP 402** (Semester 8) — Your choice from the five traditions, including the drone end the sixth semester left open. The final build, exhibited publicly beside the capstone.

**What the page is, honestly.** The Workshop is the same room it was on the first Friday, with the same tools and a master in it, and the page is still not that room. The fifteen days carry the last brief, the technique as a refresher in the tradition you chose, the critique as public defense, and the discipline of the final log and the account. A learner working alone online does the smallest real version: the last build they can finish in fifteen weeks in the tradition they liked best, shown somewhere public — a library, a shop window, a swap meet, a webpage with the eight builds on it — with the account beside it.

## Open questions for Brandon

1. Is the final Workshop exhibition the same room and night as the capstone defenses, or the same week in a different space? Days 5, 10, and 13 assume neighboring rooms in one week; the registry says "beside," which could mean either.
2. May a student's final build be made for their capstone venture's client, as the arc allows if the brief stays one thing, defended on its own — or should the last Workshop build be kept entirely separate from the capstone so that the two defenses cannot blur?
3. Who are the outside examiners for the Workshop's final bench test and defense — the capstone's examiners doing double duty, or a separate panel from the five traditions (a shaper, an amp builder, a mechanic, a cinematographer, an instrument tech)? The arc assumes the second.
