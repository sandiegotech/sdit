# Arc — WKSP 302 The Workshop

## The course in one paragraph

At the start you are leaving for the Field Term with five shipped builds behind you; by the end you have built one ocean instrument — a logger in a housing you made, measuring one variable — calibrated it against something true, deployed it off a pier or in a tide pool, left it a week in saltwater, retrieved it, and produced one plot with units that another person can read and one number you are willing to say is true. The catalog register is the sixth course of the eight-semester fabrication and design practicum; the teaching frame is a project team that ships while the Field Term runs alongside. The Workshop keeps the hands moving. The build is lighter on purpose, so the placement gets the weight.

## The made thing

The shipped project, documented: one ocean sensor — temperature, pressure, light, tide height, or salinity — in a housing the team made, deployed for at least seven days in seawater, retrieved, and reported as a plot and a one-page data note. Finished means: it came back; it has data for the whole window or an honest account of where the data stops; the plot has axes, units, and times; the calibration is in the log; the number it reports has been checked against a reference. Day 1 names the instrument and the week; Day 3 chooses the one variable; Day 4 starts the housing; Day 5 calibrates; Day 10 deploys; Day 12 retrieves. By Day 15 the plot is in the log and the instrument is on the shelf, labeled, ready to go in again.

## The unassisted examination

Day 11's week, during the deployment, tools closed: read a plot you have not seen — a bathythermograph trace, a tide record, a temperature series — and say what it shows, where it is wrong, and what you would check; then calibrate a thermometer against ice water and boiling water by hand and write the offset. Why: an instrument will tell you a number with great confidence whether or not it is true. The Workshop certifies that you can tell the difference without a machine telling you which.

## The build and the rotation

**This semester's material.** Drones and ocean sensors, the sensor end — the fifth tradition of the rotation. The drone end is introduced (Day 7) and left open for WKSP 402. Teams build one instrument: a microcontroller, a sensor, a battery, a housing, a mount, and a retrieval line. The sensor is bought; everything else is made. The smallest real version is a temperature logger in a jar, sunk a week in a tide pool, a harbor, or a bucket of seawater on a balcony.

**The client.** None assigned. The ocean is the client this semester, and the Field Term is the reason: half the team is at a placement for part of the week, so the build is sized for hands that are elsewhere. Teams that want a client — a Scripps lab, a lifeguard tower, a harbor master — may take one if the brief stays one variable, one week.

**The critique method.** Describe before you judge, and this semester the crit is remote by necessity: over three photographs and one plot, from wherever the Field Term has put you. The rule holds — describe the plot before you say whether it is good. Day 8 teaches the remote crit; every Friday after uses it.

**The exhibition.** None; the deliverable is the plot and the instrument on the shelf. Both enter the portfolio for the Year 3 gate, beside the Field Term report.

**The documentation discipline.** Three photos and a sentence, continuing — now with the plot as a fourth kind of entry. Added: the variable and the question (Day 3), the calibration log with reference values (Day 5), the power budget (Day 6), the deployment record — time, place, tie-off, tag, photo (Day 10), the retrieval record (Day 12), and the data note: one page, one plot, one number (Day 13).

**The rotation across the eight semesters.**

1. **WKSP 101** (Semester 1) — Make anything, and finish it. No assigned tradition; the room's stock pulls most first builds toward the small end of board construction.
2. **WKSP 102** (Semester 2) — Surfboard and skateboard construction. The board goes to the year-end public exhibition.
3. **WKSP 201** (Semester 3) — Amplifier and pedal electronics. The first external client: a musician who will plug it in.
4. **WKSP 202** (Semester 4) — Motorcycle mechanics. A client's machine brought back to running; the last Foundation build and the evidence for the Declaration.
5. **WKSP 301** (Semester 5) — Camera rigs and housings. A client who needs a shot they cannot get; each concentration enters the tradition through its own materials.
6. **WKSP 302** (Semester 6) — Drones and ocean sensors, the sensor end. One instrument that survives a week in saltwater and comes back with data; lighter on purpose, beside the Field Term.
7. **WKSP 401** (Semester 7) — The first-years' build. You lead a WKSP 101 team; the material is whatever they can finish.
8. **WKSP 402** (Semester 8) — Your choice from the five traditions, including the drone end the sixth semester left open. The final build, exhibited beside the capstone.

**What the page is, honestly.** The Workshop is a bench with a soldering iron, a potting compound, a pressure pot, a bucket of seawater, a pier, and someone from the Scripps instrument shop who has lost instruments and knows how. The page cannot be that. The fifteen days carry the brief, the calibration discipline, the technique in order, the remote critique prompt, and the discipline of the plot. A learner working alone online does the smallest real version: a thermometer logger in a jar, calibrated in ice and boiling water, sunk a week somewhere wet, retrieved, and plotted.
