WKSP 302 — The Workshop

The Workshop · 2 units · Semester 6 · Required core — every semester, everyone

Overview

The Workshop is the bottega — the room where mastery is transmitted through making. Every semester, every student, on a project team that ships — including this one, when the Field Term has most of your week. The build is lighter on purpose so the placement gets the weight, and its material is the fifth of the five craft traditions the eight Workshops rotate through: drones and ocean sensors, taken at the sensor end. One instrument, one variable, one week in saltwater, one plot.

The sensor is bought for a few dollars. Everything else is made: the housing that keeps the sea out, the mount, the retrieval line, the power budget that lets it sleep for 168 hours, the calibration against ice and boiling water that makes its number mean something. You deploy it off a pier or into a tide pool with a tag and a phone number, leave it a week, pull it up, photograph what grew on it, and download. The made thing is the plot — axes, units, time, gaps labeled honestly — and one number you are willing to say is true. Critique happens over three photos and a plot, from wherever the Field Term has put you. The drone end of the tradition is introduced in one day and left open for your last build.

The Workshop is a bench with potting compound, a bucket of seawater, a pier, and someone from an instrument shop who has lost instruments, and the page says so: one day a week, about ten minutes, carries the brief, the calibration discipline, the technique, the remote critique prompt, and the discipline of the plot. Then your hands take over — on the pier if you have one, and with a thermometer logger in a jar if you do not. The course ends the way every SDIT course ends: in a made thing, retrieved and reported.

The Two Registers

  • Catalog register: Project-based fabrication and design practicum — course 6 of the eight-semester Workshop sequence (WKSP 101–402); tool skills, materials, iterative making, documentation, exhibition.
  • Teaching frame: A project team that ships while the Field Term runs alongside. The Workshop keeps the hands moving.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Build an ocean instrument — sensor, microcontroller, battery, housing, mount, retrieval — that survives seven days in saltwater and comes back with data. → Workmanship
  2. Calibrate a sensor against known references, write the offsets, and say what a reading’s uncertainty is. → Judgment · Workmanship
  3. Write a power budget and a deployment plan, and prove both on the bench before trusting them in the water. → Judgment
  4. Read a plot you did not make, describe it before judging it, and tell a bad reading from a surprising one. → Judgment
  5. Produce a one-page data note — one plot, one number, gaps labeled — that someone who was not there can read. → Articulation

Each outcome maps to the five institutional outcomes (Judgment, Command of Intelligence, Workmanship, Articulation, Breadth of Mind) — the assessment spine of the degree and its accreditation evidence file.

The Made Thing

One ocean sensor in a housing the team made, calibrated, deployed for at least seven days in seawater, retrieved, and reported as a one-page data note — one plot with axes, units, and times, one number with its uncertainty — with the instrument labeled and shelved ready to go in again. It enters your portfolio beside the Field Term report and is reviewed at the Year 3 gate.

How You’re Assessed

  • The build — it came back, with data for the window or an honest account of where the data stops; the calibration and power budget are in the log.
  • The log — the variable and the question, the calibration log, the power budget, the deployment and retrieval records, three photos and a sentence per session, and the data note.
  • The unassisted bench test — a plot read cold and a thermometer calibrated by hand, with the tools closed and someone watching. The institution certifies the human, so it watches the judgment unaided.
  • Critique — described before judged, every Friday, over three photos and a plot from wherever the Field Term has put the team.
  • Portfolio review — the Workshop is assessed the way working labs are: by the instrument, retrieved, and the plot, read.
