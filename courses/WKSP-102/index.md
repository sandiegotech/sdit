# WKSP 102 — The Workshop

**The Workshop** · 2 units · Semester 2 · Required core — every semester, everyone

## Overview

The Workshop is the bottega — the room where the rest of the curriculum becomes a thing you can hold. Every semester, every student, on a project team that ships. Semester 1 taught you to finish. This semester the material is assigned for the first time: surfboard and skateboard construction, the first of five craft traditions the eight Workshops rotate through. You build a board — shaped foam and glass, wood, or seven plies of pressed maple — from template to rocker to rails to fins or trucks to finish, and you ride it before you show it.

The showing is the point. The year-end public exhibition closes Year 1, and people you do not know will stand in front of the thing you made and read sixty words about it. Critique this semester adds a stranger to the bench. Documentation adds a template drawing, a ride log, a repair log, and the placard. The Semester 1 build hangs beside the board.

The Workshop is a room with tools and a shaper in it, and the page is honest about that: one day a week on the page, about ten minutes, carries the brief, the technique, the critique prompt, and the discipline of the log. Then your hands take over — in the bay if you have one, and with a plank and a hand plane if you do not. The course ends the way every SDIT course ends: in a made thing, shown.

## The Two Registers

- **Catalog register:** Project-based fabrication and design practicum — course 2 of the eight-semester Workshop sequence (WKSP 101–402); tool skills, materials, iterative making, documentation, exhibition.
- **Teaching frame:** A project team that ships — and this semester, ships to the year-end public exhibition. People you don't know will look at the thing you made.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course you can:

1. Build a board in one of three constructions, in the right order — outline, rocker, foil, rails, fins or trucks, finish — and ride it. → *Workmanship*
2. Defend each decision on the board — rocker, width, foil, fin setup — as a trade with a consequence in the water or on the pavement. → *Judgment*
3. Take critique from a stranger, repair what the test broke, and show the difference in the log. → *Judgment · Workmanship*
4. Write a sixty-word placard and a two-minute account that a person who does not know you can follow. → *Articulation*
5. Hang and show finished work in public, and stand beside it. → *Articulation*

*Each outcome maps to the five institutional outcomes (Judgment, Command of Intelligence, Workmanship, Articulation, Breadth of Mind) — the assessment spine of the degree and its accreditation evidence file.*

## The Made Thing

A board — surfboard, handplane, or skateboard deck — finished, ridden, repaired, and shown at the year-end public exhibition beside your Semester 1 build, with a placard and a log that runs from the first template drawing to what the stranger said on opening night. It enters your portfolio and is reviewed at the annual gate.

## How You're Assessed

- **The build** — finished, ridden at least once, and repaired after the ride. A board that has not been in the water or on the pavement is not finished.
- **The log** — template drawing, three photos and a sentence per session, the ride log, the repair log, and the placard.
- **The unassisted bench test** — one technique executed with the tools closed and someone watching, and the board read aloud without notes. The institution certifies the human, so it watches the hands unaided.
- **Critique** — described before judged, every Friday, with a stranger at the bench from Week 9.
- **Portfolio review and public exhibition** — the Workshop is assessed the way working studios are: by the work, shown.
