# Arc — WKSP 102 The Workshop

## The course in one paragraph

At the start you can finish a small thing; by the end you can build a board — a surfboard, a handplane, a skateboard deck — from template to rocker to rails to fins or trucks to finish, test it in the water or on the pavement, repair what the test broke, and show it to a room of strangers with a sixty-word placard and a two-minute account. The catalog register is the second course of the eight-semester fabrication and design practicum; the teaching frame is a project team that ships, and this semester ships to the year-end public exhibition. Finishing was Semester 1's skill. Showing is this one's.

## The made thing

A board, finished and ridden, shown at the public exhibition that closes Year 1 — the registry's "shipped project, shown at the year-end public exhibition." Finished means: it has been ridden or skated at least once and the ride is in the log; every ding from the test is repaired; it hangs with a placard a stranger can read in thirty seconds; the maker stands beside it for the opening. Day 1 names the material and the exhibition; Day 2 chooses the construction (foam and glass, wood, or seven plies of maple) and the smallest real version; Day 3 starts the build with the outline and rocker. The Semester 1 build hangs beside it. By Day 15 both are on the wall and the stranger has spoken.

## The unassisted examination

Day 11's week. Tools closed, master or asked-person watching: set a fin box or mount a set of trucks true, or true one rail by hand and eye; then read the board — name its rocker, its widest point, its fin setup, and what each will do in the water — with no notes. Why this: the board is a stack of decisions, and the exam checks that you can both execute one of them with your hands and defend all of them with your mouth.

## The build and the rotation

**This semester's material.** Surfboard and skateboard construction — the first assigned tradition of the rotation. Teams choose one of three constructions: shaped foam, glassed; wood (a handplane, a hollow or chambered board, an alaia); or pressed maple veneer for a deck. The small end is allowed: a handplane or a cruiser deck is a board.

**The client.** None yet. The exhibition is the client — a room of people who do not know you, which is a harder brief than a friend and an easier one than a paying customer. Clients begin in WKSP 201.

**The critique method.** Describe before you judge, from WKSP 101, now with two additions: on Day 9 the room includes a stranger, and from Day 10 the placard is on the bench with the board and gets critiqued too. The maker still speaks last.

**The exhibition.** The year-end public exhibition closes Year 1. Day 14 is the hang (height, light, label, the maker beside it); Day 15 is the opening. The Semester 1 build hangs beside the board.

**The documentation discipline.** Three photos and a sentence, every session, continuing the Semester 1 log in the same place. Added this semester: the template drawing (Day 4), the ride log (Day 12), the repair log (Day 13), and the placard (Day 10) — sixty words, for a stranger.

**The rotation across the eight semesters.**

1. **WKSP 101** (Semester 1) — Make anything, and finish it. No assigned tradition; the room's stock pulls most first builds toward the small end of board construction.
2. **WKSP 102** (Semester 2) — Surfboard and skateboard construction. The board goes to the year-end public exhibition.
3. **WKSP 201** (Semester 3) — Amplifier and pedal electronics. The first external client: a musician who will plug it in.
4. **WKSP 202** (Semester 4) — Motorcycle mechanics. A client's machine brought back to running; the last Foundation build and the evidence for the Declaration.
5. **WKSP 301** (Semester 5) — Camera rigs and housings. A client who needs a shot they cannot get; each concentration enters the tradition through its own materials.
6. **WKSP 302** (Semester 6) — Drones and ocean sensors, the sensor end. One instrument that survives a week in saltwater and comes back with data; lighter on purpose, beside the Field Term.
7. **WKSP 401** (Semester 7) — The first-years' build. You lead a WKSP 101 team; the material is whatever they can finish.
8. **WKSP 402** (Semester 8) — Your choice from the five traditions, including the drone end the sixth semester left open. The final build, exhibited beside the capstone.

**What the page is, honestly.** The Workshop is a room with a shaping bay, a planer, a glassing rack, a deck press, and a shaper in it. The page cannot be that. The fifteen days are the brief, the technique in order, the critique prompt, and the documentation discipline. A learner working alone online does the smallest real version with what they have: a handplane from a plank and a hand plane, or a cruiser deck from a veneer kit and a clamp press, ridden once, repaired, and shown to three strangers — at a swap meet, a library table, a coffee shop wall — with the placard.
