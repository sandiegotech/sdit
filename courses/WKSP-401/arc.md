# Arc — WKSP 401 The Workshop

## The course in one paragraph

At the start you have shipped six builds and never been responsible for anyone else's; by the end you have led a team of first-years through WKSP 101 — their first build, in the same fifteen weeks, in the same room — scoped it with them rather than for them, shown a technique once and stepped back, run the Friday crit, kept them safe at the tools, proctored their bench test, and written an honest account of what leading taught you that making did not. The catalog register is the seventh course of the eight-semester fabrication and design practicum; the teaching frame is the teaching requirement made concrete — you lead first-years now, because you don't know it until you can teach it. The thing shipped is theirs. The responsibility is yours.

## The made thing

A first-year team's shipped project, led by you, and your account of leading it. The team's build is WKSP 101's: anything, finished, logged, held for the exhibition. Your artifact is two things: their finished build, which you did not make, and your account — three to five pages, in your words, with their log beside it — of the decisions you made as a leader, the ones you got wrong, and what you would do differently with the next team. Finished means: their thing ships by their Day 15; their log is complete; your account is written and read aloud to the Workshop's masters. Day 1 gives you the team and the terms; Day 2 has you read their Day 1 the week they do; Day 3 scopes their build with them; Day 6 opens your leader's log, which runs beside theirs to Day 14. The course is paced to WKSP 101, which runs in the same fall.

## The unassisted examination

Day 11's week, no notes, no AI, the masters watching: run a describe-before-you-judge crit on your team's build, start to finish, holding the room to the rules; then give a five-minute oral account of your team — where each person is, what is at risk, what you will do this week — and answer questions. Why: a leader can hide behind a slide deck as easily as a maker can hide behind a kit. The Workshop certifies that you can hold a room and know your people without a prompt.

## The build and the rotation

**This semester's material.** The first-years'. WKSP 101 assigns no tradition — make anything, and finish it — so your team's material is whatever they can finish, and most will land at the small end of board construction. You do not choose it. You do not make it. You make sure it gets made.

**The client.** The first-year team is your client, and the Institute is theirs. You answer to the Workshop's masters for the team's build and for your account.

**The critique method.** Describe before you judge, now run by you. You hold the room silent for three minutes of description, you cut off judgment that arrives early, you make sure the maker speaks last, and you give one instruction, not five. Day 5 teaches running it; the exam on Day 11 watches you do it unaided.

**The exhibition.** The team's build is held for the year-end public exhibition, which WKSP 102 carries it to next spring; your account is read aloud to the masters on Day 14. Your own final exhibition is WKSP 402.

**The documentation discipline.** Two logs. Theirs — three photos and a sentence, which you check weekly and never write for them. Yours — the leader's log: what you decided, what you did not say, who is behind and why, what the master would have done. By Day 14 the leader's log becomes the account.

**The rotation across the eight semesters.**

1. **WKSP 101** (Semester 1) — Make anything, and finish it. No assigned tradition; the room's stock pulls most first builds toward the small end of board construction.
2. **WKSP 102** (Semester 2) — Surfboard and skateboard construction. The board goes to the year-end public exhibition.
3. **WKSP 201** (Semester 3) — Amplifier and pedal electronics. The first external client: a musician who will plug it in.
4. **WKSP 202** (Semester 4) — Motorcycle mechanics. A client's machine brought back to running; the last Foundation build and the evidence for the Declaration.
5. **WKSP 301** (Semester 5) — Camera rigs and housings. A client who needs a shot they cannot get; each concentration enters the tradition through its own materials.
6. **WKSP 302** (Semester 6) — Drones and ocean sensors, the sensor end. One instrument that survives a week in saltwater and comes back with data; lighter on purpose, beside the Field Term.
7. **WKSP 401** (Semester 7) — The first-years' build. You lead a WKSP 101 team; the material is whatever they can finish.
8. **WKSP 402** (Semester 8) — Your choice from the five traditions, including the drone end the sixth semester left open. The final build, exhibited beside the capstone.

**What the page is, honestly.** The Workshop is a room with tools, a master, and now you between the master and the first-years. The page cannot be that. The fifteen days carry the leader's brief, the method of showing and stepping back, the critique as something you run, the safety you own, and the discipline of two logs. A learner working alone online does the smallest real version: lead one person newer than you — a sibling, a friend, a kid — through one small build to a finish, using WKSP 101's fifteen days as their syllabus and this course's fifteen as yours, and write the account.

## Open questions for Brandon

1. How many first-years per fourth-year? The arc assumes a team of three to four. The registry's cohort of thirty to fifty makes the ratio roughly one to one in the founding years, which changes the leader's job from running a team to pairing with one person.
2. Who grades the account — the Workshop's master alone, or a panel that includes the first-years' own view of being led? The arc has the account read aloud to the masters and leaves the team's voice to the leader's log.
3. The teaching requirement in *The Degree* lets a fourth-year lead in Workshop, Canon section, or Physical Culture. WKSP 401 assumes every fourth-year leads in Workshop. If some lead elsewhere, does WKSP 401 need an alternate build for them, or does the registry's "every fourth-year leads a Workshop team" settle it?
