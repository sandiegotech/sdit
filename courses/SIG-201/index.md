# SIG 201 — How Images Move

**Culture & Signal** · 1 unit · Semester 4 · Required core — everyone takes it

## Overview

Some images travel and most die. A horse photographed in 1878 started cinema; a photograph taken in ten minutes in 1936 fed a camp within days; a six-second loop can reach more people in a night than a studio film does in a year. This course asks why, and answers with the craft and the systems together: the frame and what it leaves out, the cut and what it does, the claim a documentary makes and whether it is true, the chain of rooms an image passes through, and the machine at the end of the chain that is tuned to keep you looking. Every day puts the real thing on the page — Muybridge's horse, Vertov's eye, Nanook's roofless igloo, Lange's *Migrant Mother*, *Earthrise*, a phenakistoscope spinning since 1833.

The second Culture & Signal unit is also San Diego's: the skate videos cut on two VCRs in this county that invented the grammar of short-form, the Hall H line where attention is paid in hours on a sidewalk, and the Navy films this town made about itself for ninety years. You learn to judge an image before you release one.

One day a week, about ten minutes a sitting. The course ends in a made thing: a short piece you shoot and cut, released into a real feed under your own name, with a written account — numbers in hand — of what the feed did with it.

## The Two Registers

- **Catalog register:** Visual media systems — cinema, short-form ecosystems, documentary, attention economics.
- **Teaching frame:** Why some images travel and others die — craft, platforms, and the economics of attention.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course you can:

1. Watch a short piece twice and say what the frame excludes, where the first cut lands and what it does, who it is for, and what it claims. → *Judgment*
2. Predict, with reasons you can be held to, whether a piece will travel in a named feed — and check the prediction against the numbers. → *Judgment*
3. Trace the path of an image from capture to audience across cinema, photojournalism, and the short-form platforms, and name what each system rewards. → *Breadth of Mind*
4. Read platform analytics honestly, separating reach from effect, and write an account of how a piece moved that a stranger would trust. → *Judgment · Breadth of Mind*

*Each outcome maps to the five institutional outcomes (Judgment, Command of Intelligence, Workmanship, Articulation, Breadth of Mind) — the assessment spine of the degree and its accreditation evidence file.*

## The Made Thing

A short visual piece — thirty seconds to three minutes, shot and cut by you — released into a real feed under your own account on a stated date, with a written account of how it moved. The account is written fourteen days after release with the platform's numbers in hand: views, completion, shares, comments, where viewers came from — and it separates what moved from what was merely counted. Your prediction from the unassisted examination sits beside the result, right or wrong. It is defended on the last day: the piece shown once, the account read aloud. It enters your portfolio and is read at the annual gate.

## How You're Assessed

- **Weekly viewing notes** against the film or photograph on the page — frame, cut, claim, audience.
- **The made thing**, released on the stated date, and **the written account**, read aloud and defended.
- **An unassisted examination** in the eleventh week: one unfamiliar piece, twice, tools closed — what it excludes, what the cut does, who it is for, and a sealed prediction of whether it will travel, opened against the real numbers three weeks later. AI tools are open for everything else; the institution certifies the human.
- Everything lands in your **portfolio**, reviewed against the five outcomes at the end-of-year gate.
