# WKSP 201 — The Workshop

**The Workshop** · 2 units · Semester 3 · Required core — every semester, everyone

## Overview

The Workshop is the bottega — the room where making is taught by making, under someone who has done it a hundred times. Every semester, every student, on a project team that ships. Year 2 brings the first outside client. The standard does not change; the person you answer to does. This semester's material is the second of the five craft traditions the eight Workshops rotate through: amplifier and pedal electronics. You build one working piece of audio gear — a fuzz, a boost, an overdrive, a small amp — for a musician who is not you, on their brief, to their deadline.

The circuit is the smaller half of the lesson. The larger half is the client: writing their brief in their words, letting their ears outrank your intentions at the bench, delivering the thing in person with a schematic they can hand to the next tech, and writing an honest account of what it cost in hours and parts. Critique this semester puts the client at the bench. Documentation adds the brief, the as-built schematic, and the account.

The Workshop is a bench with an iron, a meter, and a master at it, and the page says so plainly: one page a week, about ten minutes to read, opens the day and carries the brief, the technique, the critique prompt, and the discipline of the log. Then your hands take over — on the bench if you have one, and on a breadboard at a kitchen table if you do not. The course ends the way every SDIT course ends: in a made thing, in someone else's hands.

## The Two Registers

- **Catalog register:** Project-based fabrication and design practicum — course 3 of the eight-semester Workshop sequence (WKSP 101–402); tool skills, materials, iterative making, documentation, exhibition.
- **Teaching frame:** External clients begin. The team ships for someone who is not you, on their brief, to their deadline.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course you can:

1. Build a working audio circuit — breadboard, then hand-wired or perfboard, then boxed — with solder joints that pass a tug and a meter. → *Workmanship*
2. Read a schematic you have not seen, trace the signal path aloud, and name what each stage does. → *Workmanship · Judgment* · *Critical thinking*
3. Write a client's brief in their words with a done-test, and hold the build to it when the client's ears and your intentions disagree. → *Judgment* · *Written communication*
4. Chase noise to its source and change one thing at a time, logging each change. → *Judgment* · *Critical thinking*
5. Hand over a finished thing with an as-built schematic, a client card, and an honest account of hours and parts. → *Articulation* · *Written communication* · *Oral communication*

*Each outcome maps to the five institutional outcomes (Judgment, Command of Intelligence, Workmanship, Articulation, Breadth of Mind) — the assessment spine of the degree and its accreditation evidence file.*

## The Made Thing

A piece of amplifier or pedal electronics built for a named musician who plugs it in and uses it — boxed, labeled, delivered in person, with the as-built schematic, a one-page client card, and an account of what it cost. The client's first words after plugging in are the last entry in the log. It enters your portfolio and is reviewed at the Year 2 gate.

## How You're Assessed

- **The build** — passes signal, does what the brief says, survives a boot and a gig bag, and is in the client's hands by Week 13.
- **The log** — the signed brief, three photos and a sentence per session, test notes, the as-built schematic, the client card, and the account.
- **The unassisted bench test** — three solder joints and a cold schematic traced aloud, tools closed, someone watching. The institution certifies the human, so it watches the hands unaided.
- **Critique** — described before judged, every Friday; the client at the bench in Week 7 and at the handover in Week 13.
- **Portfolio review** — the Workshop is assessed the way working shops are: by the work, delivered.
