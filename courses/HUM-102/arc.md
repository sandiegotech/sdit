# Arc — HUM 102 Rome to the Renaissance

## The course in one paragraph

At the start, the learner thinks of the thirteen centuries between Rome and Florence as a gap. At the end, they can trace one continuous line through it: a Roman emperor writing notes to himself in a war camp, a North African bishop inventing the inner autobiography, a Florentine exile mapping the whole moral universe, a tortured diplomat writing the first honest book about power, and an apprentice in Verrocchio's workshop who never separated drawing from engineering. The catalog register is Roman, medieval, and Renaissance texts: Aurelius, Augustine, Dante, Machiavelli, da Vinci's notebooks. The teaching frame is the Double Feature, beginning with Aurelius beside *Dogtown and Z-Boys* and ending with da Vinci's notebooks as the founding model of the artist-engineer. The learner leaves able to read a private notebook as a form, to say what a text asks of its reader, and to defend the claim that art and engineering were one job before the industrial university divided them.

## The made thing

A made response to this material in one of four forms: an essay (1,200 to 2,000 words), a short film (under six minutes), a recorded piece, or a designed object with a one-page note. This semester the brief has one extra constraint, taken from the course itself: the response must be made with what is at hand. Marcus wrote in a tent, the Z-Boys skated drained pools, Dilla made *Donuts* from a hospital bed, Leonardo drew water in a notebook. The learner names their constraint on the first page and works inside it. "Finished" means the thing exists, can be experienced without the maker present, names its text, and states its constraint. It is defended in seminar the week after Day 15: three minutes to present, seven of questions, peers first. It enters the portfolio and is read at the end-of-year gate; since Semester 2 ends in the public exhibition, the made thing may also be shown there.

Day 4 introduces it: Marcus on the shortness of time, and the course's instruction to make the thing now, with what is in reach. Day 8 scopes it: at the start of the *Inferno*, the learner names their text, their form, and their constraint in three sentences. Day 11 starts it: on the mountain of Purgatory, where effort improves you, the first draft, cut, demo, or sketch is due within the week. Day 15 sets the defense in the workshop where Leonardo painted an angel into his master's commission.

## The unassisted examination

In the week of Day 13, thirty minutes, tools closed: state Machiavelli's argument in Chapter 17 of *The Prince* (fear and love, and the one thing a ruler must avoid) accurately enough that he would sign it, then answer it in your own voice. Machiavelli is the right text for this because he is the course's most uncomfortable author, and the degree needs to see that the learner can hold an argument they may dislike without distorting it, and then reply to it without help. The Day 15 defense is also unaided.

## Open questions for Brandon

1. *Donuts* as the Aurelius album: the argument is constraint and mortality, a man finishing the thing in front of him in a hospital bed. If you want the album to stay inside Dogtown's own sound, the alternative is Black Flag's *Damaged* (Hermosa Beach, 1981). Which?
2. Virgil gets one day (Day 9) as Dante's guide, using the Lectio *Aeneid*, though the catalog register does not name him. It makes the Rome-to-Florence line literal. Keep it, or give the day back to Dante?
3. *Man with a Movie Camera* is public domain and could be embedded in full on Day 14 or 15, the only film in the Canon's first four courses that can be. Should the course make a point of that, and show a sequence on the page?
