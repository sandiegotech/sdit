# WKSP 301 — The Workshop

**The Workshop** · 2 units · Semester 5 · Required core — every semester, everyone

## Overview

The Workshop is the bottega — the room where mastery is transmitted through making, under someone who has done it before. Every semester, every student, on a project team that ships. Year 3 of the Workshop runs beside your first depth studios, and its material is the fourth of the five craft traditions the eight Workshops rotate through: camera rigs and housings. A photographer or filmmaker brings a shot they have given up on — in the water, on a board, on a bike, from a pole. Your team builds the thing between the camera and the world that gets it.

This is the tradition every concentration can enter through its own door, which is why it sits here, just after the Declaration. Media builds the operating end: where the camera goes and how it moves. Design builds the part a person holds: the handle, the latch, the housing opened cold and wet with one hand. Engineering builds the part that must work on its own: the seal, the trigger, the firmware. Teams are mixed; the crit goes around the table with each person describing another concentration's decisions; the test protocol is written before the first part is cut; and the rig is judged by one thing only — whether the client's shot is in the footage.

The Workshop is a bench with O-ring stock, a bucket, a pier, and someone who has flooded a housing, and the page says so: one day a week, about ten minutes, carries the brief, the test protocol, the technique, the critique prompt, and the discipline of the test log. Then your hands take over — in the shop if you have one, and with a dry bag, a frame, and a bucket if you do not. The course ends the way every SDIT course ends: in a made thing, in a client's hands, with the shot to prove it.

## The Two Registers

- **Catalog register:** Project-based fabrication and design practicum — course 5 of the eight-semester Workshop sequence (WKSP 101–402); tool skills, materials, iterative making, documentation, exhibition.
- **Teaching frame:** The Workshop in your concentration's materials, with a client — boards, electronics, rigs, drones, sensors; the rotation continues.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course you can:

1. Build a camera rig or housing that holds a real camera, balances in the hand, comes back dry, and gets the shot named in the brief. → *Workmanship*
2. Write a test protocol before the build — dry, weighted, bucket, pool, sea, each with a pass line — and log every result. → *Judgment · Workmanship*
3. Inspect a housing you did not build, name its failure modes, and say which to test first. → *Judgment*
4. Enter a shared build through your concentration's materials, and describe another concentration's decisions at the bench as if they were yours. → *Judgment*
5. Document the rig from several views, with a test log and the footage, so the next builder could repeat it. → *Articulation*

*Each outcome maps to the five institutional outcomes (Judgment, Command of Intelligence, Workmanship, Articulation, Breadth of Mind) — the assessment spine of the degree and its accreditation evidence file.*

## The Made Thing

A camera rig or housing built for a named photographer or filmmaker, tested to a written protocol, taken into the water or onto the mount with a real camera, and delivered with a card, drawings from several views, the test log, and thirty seconds of the client's footage showing the shot they named in Week 3. It enters your portfolio and is reviewed at the Year 3 gate.

## How You're Assessed

- **The build** — dry, balanced, holding the client's camera, and used by the client for the shot in the brief. The footage is the done-test.
- **The log** — the brief in the client's units, the test protocol and results, three photos and a sentence per session, drawings from several views, and the footage.
- **The unassisted bench test** — a stranger's housing inspected and its failure modes named, then a housing sealed and passed, with the tools closed and someone watching. The institution certifies the human, so it watches the hands unaided.
- **Critique** — described before judged, every Friday, with each concentration describing another's decisions in Weeks 7 and 14.
- **Portfolio review** — the Workshop is assessed the way working shops are: by the work, used.
