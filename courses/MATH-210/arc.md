# Arc — MATH 210 Linear Algebra

## The course in one paragraph

At the start, the learner edits photographs and mixes audio with sliders and does not know that every slider is a matrix. At the end, the learner can take a grid of numbers — a photograph, a stereo file, a layer of a neural network — and rotate it, blend it, project it, compress it, and undo it by hand; say what a transformation destroys and what it keeps; find the directions it leaves alone; and open a working model and recognize the same operations inside. That is the catalog register, vector spaces, matrices, linear transformations, eigenvalues. The teaching frame is images and audio — every photograph, every mix session, every AI model is matrices — and the course is the secret on-ramp to machine learning: by the end the learner reads a model's insides because they have built the operations themselves.

## The made thing

An image or audio transformation built by hand from matrix operations, then found again inside a working model. The learner chooses one: a blur, a sharpen, an edge detector, a color-space change, a rotation with perspective, a mid/side stereo widener, a compression that keeps the biggest singular values. They build it as explicit matrices in a notebook — no library call that hides the multiplication — apply it to their own photograph or recording, and show before and after. Then they open a small working model (a pretrained convolutional network's first layer, or a learned audio filter) and locate the same operation: the kernel that is their blur, the weights that are their edge detector, the basis that is their color change. "Finished" means the by-hand code, the before-and-after, the model's weights shown beside the learner's matrix, and a two-page account of what matches, what does not, and why. It is presented and defended on Day 15: the room names a pixel or a sample and the learner traces it through every matrix. It enters the portfolio.

Day 1 introduces it (a photograph is a spreadsheet; you will change one by hand). Day 6 scopes it (choose image or audio, and the transformation). Day 9 starts it (the first matrices applied to your own file). Day 14 finds it in the model. Day 15 is the defense.

## The unassisted examination

Two sittings, tools closed. After Day 8: vector addition and scaling, a dot product and a projection, a two-by-two transformation drawn before it is computed, a matrix product in the right order, a three-unknown system solved by elimination, a determinant and what its sign and size mean. After Day 14: a change of basis carried through, an eigenvector found and checked, the rank and null space of a small matrix named with what they destroy, and a least-squares line set up. The hand computation is examined unaided because the learner must be able to catch a machine's shape error in one glance; the geometric questions are examined unaided because reading what a transformation does is the judgment the course exists to build.
