ART 410 — Generative Media & Creative Code

The Depths · 4 units · Semester 7 · Depth studio — Media & Design · the Crossing for others

Overview

The medium that did not exist ten years ago, handled with the oldest discipline there is: taste. Procedural systems and machine intelligence are raw material here, the way paint and film stock were material before. The artist’s job moves up a level — to the rules and to the choosing — and this course is where that sentence stops being a slogan and becomes a practice with a critique method: write a rule you can sign, run it a thousand times, lay the outputs on the floor, keep ten, and say why.

The thing to look at each day is a rule-made thing: a snowflake, Mozart’s dice tables, a Jacquard card, a Persian tiling that never repeats, Conway’s glider gun, a 1704 tiling, a neural network playing death metal forever, Duchamp’s urinal, Dürer’s letters with their construction printed beside them. A machine-learning model enters on Day 9 as one more very large rule set that someone else wrote; you learn to use it the way a photographer uses a camera, and to say exactly what in the output is yours. This is a depth studio: a room with a master in it — an artist who has made and shown generative work and has curated — and the page is the brief. Harold Cohen built AARON up the road at UC San Diego for forty years; you are in that line.

It ends in a made thing, exhibited: a curated set with the rules on the wall beside it, and a defense of the choosing.

The Two Registers

  • Catalog register: Computational art — procedural and generative methods, AI-assisted media.
  • Teaching frame: AI and procedure as artistic material — the newest medium, handled with taste.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course you can:

  1. Author a generative system — in code, in a written procedure, or as an architecture around a model — whose rules another person can run cold and get recognizably the same work. → Workmanship
  2. Use noise, emergence, growth, and machine-learning models as material, and state for any output what the system made and what you did. → Command of Intelligence
  3. Curate a body of a thousand outputs to an exhibited set, and defend the selection and its sequence. → Judgment
  4. Infer the rule from a system’s outputs, and write a generative procedure on paper without a machine. → Judgment · Command of Intelligence
  5. Take and publish a position on authorship, training data, and consent for your own work, and defend it to a stranger. → Judgment

Each outcome maps to the five institutional outcomes (Judgment, Command of Intelligence, Workmanship, Articulation, Breadth of Mind) — the assessment spine of the degree and its accreditation evidence file.

The Made Thing

A generative work where the system is yours: one system in one medium — image, sound, text, or motion — run to a body of at least a thousand outputs, curated to an exhibited set, with the rules published beside the outputs and a 200-word curatorial statement. Finished means a classmate has run the system without you in the room and produced outputs that are the same work and not the same pictures; the set is chosen and sequenced; the rules are on the wall. It is exhibited at the semester’s public show and enters your portfolio for the Year 4 gate — and often becomes a capstone proposal.

How You’re Assessed

  • The rules, written as one paragraph on Day 4 before any code, frozen and published on Day 12.
  • Three critiques: a thousand outputs on the floor (Day 8), the authorship critique (Day 11), and the cold run by a classmate (Day 12) — if they cannot run it, it is not finished.
  • The unassisted examination (Day 10): a generative procedure written on paper that a classmate executes with dice and a deck, then curated; and twenty outputs from a system you have never seen, with twenty minutes to write its rule. The AI tools are open as material every other day; the author of rules is examined authoring rules without one.
  • The exhibition: the curated set, the rules beside it, the statement, and the defense of the choosing.
  • Everything lands in your portfolio, reviewed against the five outcomes at the end-of-year gate.
