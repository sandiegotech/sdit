# RHET 210 — Epistemic Self-Defense

**Rhetoric & Epistemics** · 4 units · Semester 4 · Required core — everyone takes it

## Overview

Your information environment is built by people who want things from you. Some of them are honest. The skill this course trains is telling which, and it is taught as a martial art: logic, statistical reasoning, cognitive bias, and media literacy, each learned as a move you make on a real piece of persuasion. Who put this here? Does the conclusion follow, and are the premises so? Out of how many? Where is the control? What do I want to be true? Who said it first?

The cases are historical and public domain, because there the record is complete and the stakes are cold: a 1917 recruiting poster that printed four million copies, John Snow's cholera map, Nightingale's wedges, the Cottingley Fairies and the man who believed in them, Bernays manufacturing a news event in 1929, Capra cutting the enemy's own film into an argument. You learn the moves where you can check the answer, then use them where you cannot. Models are open as fallacy-finders and provenance tools, with one standing rule: you check every finding and write down the check. In the last weeks you turn the machine around — ask it to write the persuasion, then tear down what it wrote.

One day a week, about ten minutes a sitting, plus a weekly log of claims met in the wild. The course ends in a made thing: a forensic teardown of one real piece of persuasion, defended against a peer whose job is to show you were unfair.

## The Two Registers

- **Catalog register:** Logic, statistical reasoning, cognitive bias, media literacy.
- **Teaching frame:** Propaganda, information warfare, and the engineered feed — how to know what's true when the environment is adversarial.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course you can:

1. Take any argument apart into premises and conclusion, and judge separately whether it follows and whether it is true. → *Judgment*
2. Read a number, a chart, or a causal claim and ask the questions that test it — the denominator, the baseline, the control — in words, before any formula. → *Judgment*
3. Name the techniques a piece of persuasion is using on you, including the ones that work on your own wants, and show the evidence on the page that it is using them. → *Judgment*
4. Trace a claim to its first appearance and write out its chain of custody, using models for the search and checking everything they return. → *Command of Intelligence*
5. Use a model adversarially — have it build the strongest case for a position, then tear that case down — and document what the machine did to persuade. → *Command of Intelligence*

*Each outcome maps to the five institutional outcomes (Judgment, Command of Intelligence, Workmanship, Articulation, Breadth of Mind) — the assessment spine of the degree and its accreditation evidence file.*

## The Made Thing

A forensic teardown of one real piece of persuasion — a poster, a speech, a chart, an ad, a feed — documented as a dossier of eight to twelve pages: the piece itself; every claim it makes, tagged by kind and by who benefits; every technique it uses, named and evidenced; the truth of the matter, each factual claim checked to a primary source with the chain of custody written out; a machine test, in which a model wrote the persuader's case afresh and had it torn down the same way; and a one-page verdict. It is defended before the seminar, with one peer assigned as the persuader's advocate, and enters your portfolio for the Year 2 gate.

## How You're Assessed

- **A weekly claim log** — three claims met in the wild, each tagged by kind, evidence, and beneficiary — returned marked every week.
- **The dossier in stages** — case statement, claims inventory, techniques inventory, truth of the matter, machine test — each line-edited and returned, with every source you cite opened and read by the editor.
- **The teardown defended**, in seminar, against the persuader's advocate.
- **An unassisted examination in reasoning.** AI tools are open for fallacy-finding and provenance, with every finding checked by you; in Week 11 you mark an unseen persuasive text, solve a base-rate problem in words, and judge an argument twice, in sixty minutes with the tools closed. A model can do all three for you, which is exactly why the institution must watch you do them alone.
- Everything lands in your **portfolio**, reviewed against the five outcomes at the end-of-year gate.
