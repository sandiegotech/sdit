The Schedule

What a week looks like.

15-week semesters · one course each weekday · about 48 hours of work a week, full time · dated from 4 January 2027

The Shape
A semester

Fifteen weeks, five or six courses, about sixteen units. The same courses run all semester, so each one builds week over week. The semester ends at a gate: the same questions you answered on Day 0, answered again.

A week

One course each weekday. Monday is always the books, Friday is always the Workshop; the middle of the week changes with the semester, and each semester's week is laid out below. One unit is three hours of work a week, so a full semester is about 48 hours: the reading, the problem sets, the hours at the bench, and the day's page that opens each of them.

A day

The page is ten minutes. The day is the course's hours. Each weekday opens with that course's page: one thing to experience, one idea, one question to carry. Then the work it points to: the reading behind the doors, the problems, the build. A four-unit course is twelve hours that week; the page is the door, not the course.

Your pace

The dates are the full-time pace. Taking longer is allowed, and the next day is there whenever you come back; your place is kept on this device and in your account. Skipping the work is not a faster pace, it is a different thing, and the Portfolio shows the difference.

A Day's Page
One thing
to experience

A photograph, a piece of music, a few minutes of film — on the page, not linked away.

One idea

A few hundred words, with the key passage right there, and the catalog register named once so a transcript reader can place it.

One question

Carried with you, and written down: your answer goes into your Portfolio. Then the doors: the reading, the film, the person to ask, which are where the week's hours go.

Ten minutes gets you through the page. It does not get you through the course, and nothing here says it does.
