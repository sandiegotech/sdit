How every kind of made thing, and every unassisted examination, is judged. Each rubric has four to six criteria, each mapped to the institutional outcomes it evidences, and four levels described in a sentence each.

rubrics

  • id
    made-response
    name
    The made response (the Canon)
    applies_to
    An essay, short film, recorded piece, or designed object answering a text, film, and album, defended in seminar.
    criteria
    • name
      Reading
      outcomes
      • breadth
      levels
      • Summarizes the text; the argument is not yet reconstructed.
      • Reconstructs the argument with gaps or a misreading.
      • Reconstructs the argument accurately, with evidence from the text.
      • Reconstructs it as its author would, and finds what it leaves unsaid.
    • name
      The connection
      outcomes
      • judgment
      • breadth
      levels
      • Places the three works side by side without joining them.
      • Names a connection that rests on one of the three.
      • Defends one idea across text, film, and album with evidence from each.
      • Makes the three works illuminate each other in a way a reader had not seen.
    • name
      The making
      outcomes
      • craft
      levels
      • The response is unfinished or does not stand without its maker.
      • It is finished; the form fights the idea.
      • It is finished, and the form carries the idea.
      • The form is the argument; it could not have been made another way.
    • name
      The defense
      outcomes
      • articulation
      levels
      • Cannot yet say what the work claims.
      • States the claim; questions knock it over.
      • States and holds the claim under seminar questions.
      • Uses the questions to sharpen the claim in the room.
  • id
    quantitative-study
    name
    The quantitative study (Mathematics and Science)
    applies_to
    A real motion, sound, image, wave, board, or forecast, measured, modeled, and defended.
    criteria
    • name
      Measurement
      outcomes
      • judgment
      • craft
      levels
      • Data are missing, borrowed, or uncollected.
      • Data are collected; method and error are not stated.
      • Data are collected with a stated method and an estimate of error.
      • The measurement is designed so its error can be bounded and checked.
    • name
      The model
      outcomes
      • judgment
      levels
      • A formula is applied without showing why it fits.
      • The model fits the data; its assumptions are unstated.
      • The model fits, its assumptions are stated, and the fit is shown.
      • The model's limits are found by testing where it breaks.
    • name
      The mathematics
      outcomes
      • judgment
      levels
      • Steps contain errors that change the result.
      • Steps are correct with minor slips.
      • Steps are correct and each is justified.
      • The mathematics is economical and chosen for the physics.
    • name
      Physical before formal
      outcomes
      • breadth
      • craft
      levels
      • The notation stands alone; the physical thing is absent.
      • The physical thing is named but not used.
      • Every quantity is tied to something that can be seen or felt.
      • A reader without the notation could follow the argument from the physical thing.
    • name
      The defense
      outcomes
      • articulation
      levels
      • Cannot explain the setup.
      • Explains the answer, not the setup.
      • Explains the setup and the answer, and takes questions.
      • Anticipates the strongest objection and answers it first.
  • id
    working-system
    name
    The working system (Computation and Engineering)
    applies_to
    A program, model, instrument, or autonomous system that runs, with its verification and failure analysis.
    criteria
    • name
      It works
      outcomes
      • craft
      levels
      • It does not run, or runs only in the maker's hands.
      • It runs on the demonstrated case.
      • It runs on cases the maker did not choose.
      • It runs in the world, under conditions it was not tuned for.
    • name
      Direction and verification
      outcomes
      • intelligence
      • judgment
      levels
      • Machine-written work is accepted unread.
      • Machine-written work is read but not tested.
      • Every delegated piece is specified, tested, and logged.
      • The verification would catch a subtle error a reviewer planted.
    • name
      Failure analysis
      outcomes
      • judgment
      levels
      • Failures are not reported.
      • Failures are listed without causes.
      • Each failure has a cause and a consequence.
      • The analysis predicts failures not yet seen.
    • name
      Measurement
      outcomes
      • judgment
      • craft
      levels
      • Claims of speed, accuracy, or reliability are unmeasured.
      • Measured once, without a baseline.
      • Measured against a baseline, with method stated.
      • Measured so another builder could reproduce the number.
    • name
      The account
      outcomes
      • articulation
      levels
      • A reader cannot tell what the system does.
      • A reader can tell what it does, not why it is built so.
      • A reader can tell what, why, and what was traded away.
      • The account would let a stranger maintain it.
  • id
    writing-portfolio
    name
    The writing portfolio (Rhetoric)
    applies_to
    Revised essays, first drafts beside finals, line-edited.
    criteria
    • name
      The claim
      outcomes
      • judgment
      • articulation
      levels
      • No claim can be found.
      • A claim is present but hedged into nothing.
      • A single claim, stated early, holds the essay together.
      • The claim is one a reasonable reader resists, and earns its place.
    • name
      Evidence
      outcomes
      • judgment
      levels
      • Assertion without support.
      • Support that is general or borrowed.
      • Particular evidence, rightly placed.
      • Evidence chosen so the reader sees it before being told.
    • name
      The sentence
      outcomes
      • articulation
      levels
      • Sentences obscure their meaning.
      • Clear, with padding and abstraction.
      • Plain, short, and exact; every sentence does work.
      • The prose has a voice and nothing to cut.
    • name
      Revision
      outcomes
      • articulation
      • craft
      levels
      • The final repeats the draft.
      • Marks were answered one by one; the essay is the same.
      • The final is a different, better essay than the draft.
      • The distance between draft and final teaches something about writing.
  • id
    forensic-teardown
    name
    The forensic teardown (Epistemics)
    applies_to
    A documented teardown of a real piece of persuasion, defended against a persuader's advocate.
    criteria
    • name
      The claims
      outcomes
      • judgment
      levels
      • The piece is described, not dismantled.
      • Main claims are listed; their kinds are not.
      • Every claim is listed and tagged by kind and evidence.
      • Implied claims are surfaced and tagged as well.
    • name
      The techniques
      outcomes
      • judgment
      • intelligence
      levels
      • Techniques are named loosely.
      • Techniques are named and located.
      • Each technique is named, located, and its effect explained.
      • The design of the whole is reconstructed from its parts.
    • name
      The truth of the matter
      outcomes
      • judgment
      levels
      • The verdict rests on opinion.
      • The verdict rests on one source.
      • Each claim is checked against sources a skeptic would accept.
      • Where the truth cannot be settled, the uncertainty is stated and bounded.
    • name
      Sources
      outcomes
      • judgment
      • articulation
      levels
      • Sources are missing.
      • Sources are listed without assessment.
      • Each source is assessed for who made it and why.
      • The source trail would survive an adversarial review.

      levels

    • name

      • breadth
